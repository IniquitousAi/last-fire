# Last Fire: the lore (from his 20 answers, 2026-10-19; each answer word for word in ANSWERS.md)

North star: **Arcane + Destiny + Maze Runner had a baby.** Dark and neon. Dualities, push and pull. Dry dark humor, existential dread, suspense, mystery.

## The world
Nobody knows what happened. Every survivor has a theory, and all of them could be wrong. The camp lives around the fire; people work the near dark every day for what the camp needs (light, salvage, people, more), and most come back. Past the dark, no one has ever come back. Nothing happens to the ones it takes: they simply stop being anywhere. The camp has a form for it, in triplicate.

## The truth
Everything is a choice, and Chaos always wins. Nobody wants to admit it, because of ego, and ego is the villain: the dark does not care; the harm comes from people who cannot admit the truth.

The camp copes by brutal honesty, in theory: every morning the odds go up on a board ("Survival today: 81%. Have a nice day."). In practice, the leaders soften the numbers when they are bad, and the truth is on the board only if someone goes and checks. When you prove they lied, nobody cares. You are right, alone, and a little less welcome at dinner. Knowing the truth gives you an edge, and peace. Nothing else.

## You
You used to hide the odds. One night you saw the real number, the camp's chance over the long run, and you could not unsee it. It was not quite zero: a decimal with too many zeros, the chance that something is past the dark.

## The game
**Raising the number is the game.** Nobody knows what is out there. Everything you build, learn and survive nudges that decimal up on the honest board, era by era. The simulators compute it from the camp's real state, so it is never a fake progress bar. The game starts primitive and grows in eras (scavenged tech, then light tech, then the dark's own), each deeper into the lore, launched when the data says so (real player feedback weighs more than the robots). Whoever cares enough to check knows the odds; better tech (a wrist screen) shows them anywhere.

## The humor
In the camp's paperwork (forms, signs, quotas) and in the board's footnotes (deadpan fine print under the honest odds).

## The first minute (decided by logic; his "cant decide")
Your old desk in the leaders' room: the softened odds you signed, and the real one beside it with too many zeros. You get up and go check. The camp at dawn, the board going up. The dark comes on your first night.
